Cyber Risk Baseline Template for Students

A defensible cyber-risk baseline starts with a fictional or authorized scope, inventories assets and dependencies, links plausible events to observable impacts, records safeguards and evidence gaps, then assigns a few prioritized actions with owners and proof of completion.

Curriculum activity: Build a Defensible Cyber-Risk Baseline

This note helps you create: Cyber-Risk Baseline for a Synthetic Small Business

A practical, evidence-led method

  1. Define a fictional or explicitly authorized organization, its decision owner, and the narrow scope of the review.
  2. List the important services, information, dependencies, and responsibilities using only facts that can be evidenced.
  3. Describe plausible adverse events and connect each one to an affected asset and observable impact.
  4. Record current safeguards, evidence gaps, assumptions, and limitations without treating missing evidence as protection.
  5. Prioritize a small set of actions by risk, effort, reversibility, owner, and the evidence that would show progress.

Safety boundary

Use only fictional, synthetic, owned, or explicitly authorized information. Do not inspect live third-party systems, collect personal data, or present the review as certification.

Copy-ready artifact outline

Start a document named Cyber-Risk Baseline for a Synthetic Small Business and use these five sections. Replace every prompt with evidence from your owned, synthetic, or explicitly authorized lab.

  1. Context and decision

    State the fictional or authorized organization, decision owner, review date, narrow scope, exclusions, and the decision this baseline should support.

  2. Asset and dependency register

    For each important service, information set, or dependency, record its owner, evidence reference, and the consequence of loss, disclosure, or unauthorized change.

  3. Risk statements

    Write each risk as an adverse event, the affected asset, and an observable impact. Label assumptions and uncertainty separately from evidenced facts.

  4. Safeguards and evidence gaps

    List each current safeguard with the evidence that supports it. Put unknown, outdated, or missing evidence in a separate gap column.

  5. Prioritized action register

    For each proposed action, record the risk addressed, owner, effort, reversibility, target date, and evidence that would demonstrate completion.

Before you call it complete

  • Authorization and scope are visible.
  • Every conclusion points to evidence or is labeled as an assumption.
  • Sensitive details are removed or redacted.
  • Cleanup, rollback, and remaining limitations are recorded.

What to prove

Aim to produce a reviewable artifact such as Cyber-Risk Baseline for a Synthetic Small Business. Keep facts, assumptions, limits, and redactions visible so a reviewer can understand what the work demonstrates and what it does not.

Primary references

Check your decisions against these official sources. Record the version or access date you used because guidance can change.

  • The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0

    Use its outcomes and Profiles to organize assets, existing safeguards, gaps, priorities, and risk decisions.

  • NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1 - Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments

    Use its structured method to connect threats, vulnerabilities, likelihood, impact, evidence, uncertainty, and response priorities.
