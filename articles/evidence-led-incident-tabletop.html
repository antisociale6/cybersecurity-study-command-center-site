Curriculum activity: Run an Evidence-Led Incident Tabletop

This note helps you create: Synthetic Incident Response Tabletop and After-Action Review

A practical, evidence-led method

  1. Create a fictional incident scenario with synthetic evidence, a clear learning objective, participants, boundaries, and stop conditions.
  2. Define roles, escalation paths, decision authority, communication expectations, and evidence-preservation responsibilities.
  3. Present scenario updates in stages and require each decision to cite known facts, uncertainty, risk, owner, and next evidence need.
  4. Compare containment and recovery options by safety, reversibility, business impact, evidence preservation, and approval requirements.
  5. Produce an after-action review with strengths, gaps, assigned improvements, due dates, and a plan to verify completion.

Safety boundary

Keep every identity, organization, event, indicator, and record fictional or synthetic. Do not use active incident data, contact real responders, or trigger operational security actions.

Copy-ready artifact outline

Start a document named Synthetic Incident Response Tabletop and After-Action Review and use these five sections. Replace every prompt with evidence from your owned, synthetic, or explicitly authorized lab.

  1. Scenario and exercise boundary

    State the fictional incident, learning objective, synthetic evidence, participants, authorization, exclusions, stop conditions, and exercise timeline.

  2. Roles and escalation map

    Record fictional roles, decision authority, escalation paths, communication expectations, evidence-preservation duties, and unavailable capabilities.

  3. Inject and decision log

    For each staged update, capture known facts, uncertainty, decision, rationale, risk, owner, time, and the next evidence needed.

  4. Containment and recovery comparison

    Compare options by safety, reversibility, operational impact, evidence preservation, approval requirement, expected result, and rollback condition.

  5. After-action improvement plan

    List strengths, gaps, assigned improvement, accountable owner, due date, completion evidence, and a follow-up date to verify the change.

Before you call it complete

  • Authorization and scope are visible.
  • Every conclusion points to evidence or is labeled as an assumption.
  • Sensitive details are removed or redacted.
  • Cleanup, rollback, and remaining limitations are recorded.

What to prove

Aim to produce a reviewable artifact such as Synthetic Incident Response Tabletop and After-Action Review. Keep facts, assumptions, limits, and redactions visible so a reviewer can understand what the work demonstrates and what it does not.

Primary references

  • CISA Cybersecurity Tabletop Exercise Package Documents

    Use its planner and facilitator handbooks, feedback forms, and after-action template to structure roles, injects, decisions, and improvements.

  • NIST SP 800-84 - Guide to Test, Training, and Exercise Programs

    Use its design, conduct, and evaluation method to set objectives, run a bounded exercise, and convert observations into corrective actions.
