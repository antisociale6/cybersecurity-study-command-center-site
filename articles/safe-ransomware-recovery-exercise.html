Safe Ransomware Recovery Exercise Without Malware

Create synthetic files with separate source, backup, impact-copy, restore, and evidence locations. Alter only the disposable copy, restore into a new empty location, and compare integrity, completeness, elapsed time, objectives, and documented limitations.

Curriculum activity: Prove Recovery Without Running Ransomware

This note helps you create: Measured Ransomware-Resilience Restore Exercise

A practical, evidence-led method

  1. Create a small synthetic dataset and separate disposable source, backup, impact-copy, restored, and evidence locations.
  2. Record baseline inventory and integrity evidence for the known-good source and backup before the exercise begins.
  3. Represent impact only by changing a disposable copy and verify that the source and separated backup remain untouched.
  4. Restore into a new empty location and compare integrity, completeness, elapsed time, and documented recovery objectives.
  5. Write a recovery report covering evidence, unmet objectives, assumptions, separation limits, ownership, and the next safe test.

Safety boundary

Never create, obtain, execute, or simulate ransomware or other malware. Use only synthetic data and disposable copies in an owned local folder, preserving the known-good source and backup.

Copy-ready artifact outline

Start a document named Measured Ransomware-Resilience Restore Exercise and use these five sections. Replace every prompt with evidence from your owned, synthetic, or explicitly authorized lab.

  1. Exercise layout and safety boundary

    Record the synthetic dataset plus separate source, backup, disposable impact-copy, empty restore, and evidence locations, with owner and stop conditions.

  2. Baseline and backup evidence

    Capture file inventory, integrity values, timestamps, backup separation, restore instructions, and the recovery objectives before representing any impact.

  3. Safe impact representation

    Describe the harmless change made only to the disposable copy and prove that the known-good source and separated backup remained untouched.

  4. Restore measurement

    Record start and finish time, restored location, inventory comparison, integrity comparison, completeness, errors, and whether each objective was met.

  5. Recovery decision and next test

    Document evidence, unmet objectives, assumptions, separation limits, accountable owner, corrective action, and the next safe exercise date.
