Threat-Model a Small Web Service

A design-stage workflow for mapping assets, actors, data flows, trust boundaries, and reviewable security decisions for a small web service.

Useful when searching for: beginner web service threat model example

A practical, evidence-led method

  1. Describe a fictional or locally owned service, its users, business purpose, and the decisions the model should inform.
  2. Map components, data stores, external dependencies, actors, and data flows at a level a reviewer can understand.
  3. Mark trust boundaries and identify where identity, sensitive information, or administrative authority changes hands.
  4. Write plausible misuse or failure scenarios and connect each scenario to a specific asset and boundary.
  5. Document proposed controls, residual uncertainty, an accountable owner, and evidence needed for later validation.

Safety boundary

Keep the exercise at design-review level and use a fictional or owned local service. Do not probe public services, enumerate real users, or include credentials or personal information.

What to prove

Aim to produce a reviewable artifact such as Design-Stage Threat Model for a Local Appointment Service. Keep facts, assumptions, limits, and redactions visible so a reviewer can understand what the work demonstrates and what it does not.
